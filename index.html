Biography

About

Lior Rokach is a Professor of Artificial Intelligence at the Institute for Applied AI Research, Faculty of Computer and Information Science, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev (BGU). His research group develops machine-learning algorithms and applies them to healthcare, cyber security and bioinformatics. In 2026, on the recommendation of the UN Secretary-General, he was elected one of the 40 members of the Independent International Scientific Panel on Artificial Intelligence. He is also a Research Collaborator in the Department of Hematology at the Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minnesota.

He has published more than 400 peer-reviewed papers in journals, conference proceedings and edited volumes, and has written six books, several of them in more than one edition. These include Data Mining with Decision Trees (World Scientific, 2008; 2nd ed. 2015) and Ensemble Learning: Pattern Classification Using Ensemble Methods (World Scientific, 2010; 2nd ed. 2019). He co-edited the Recommender Systems Handbook (Springer, 2011, 2015, 2022) and The Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery Handbook (Springer, 2005, 2010; 3rd ed. 2023 as Machine Learning for Data Science Handbook). Three of his books have been translated into Chinese. A Stanford University study places him among the top 0.1% of AI researchers by career-long impact.

His main methodological contribution is in ensemble learning, recognized by BGU’s Toronto Prize for Academic Excellence in Research (2012). He has also designed recommender systems deployed on large e-commerce sites that serve millions of users. Papers he advised and co-authored received the Best Research Paper Award at ACM CIKM 2019, the Best Student Paper Award at ACM UMAP 2018 and the Best Paper Runner-up Award at IEEE ICDM 2021. In 2018 he spoke at the World Economic Forum in Davos on adversarial attacks and their implications for AI.

He founded BGU’s Machine Learning Laboratory in 2006. The lab has since carried out more than two dozen funded projects for sponsors that include Deutsche Telekom, DARPA, Intel, Audi, IBM, EMC, Lockheed Martin, KBC Bank, General Motors, the EU Horizon 2020 programme, eBay and Rafael. He chaired the Department of Software and Information Systems Engineering from 2017 to 2020 and was Vice Dean for Research of the Faculty of Engineering from 2023 to 2025.

Before joining BGU he spent more than 15 years in software design and development as co-founder, CTO, VP of R&D and chief architect in start-ups and public companies. He co-founded Kamoon, YooChoose (acquired by eZ Systems) and Deepchecks (acquired by Check Point). He served in the Israel Defense Forces’ Intelligence Unit 8200. He lives in Omer with his wife, Ronit Flint, and their four sons.

Research

Research areas

The group works on learning algorithms and on the conditions under which they can be trusted in deployment: accuracy, stability, robustness to attack and interpretability.

Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare

Clinical prediction and decision support built from electronic health records and other real-world data: risk models for hematologic malignancies such as CLL and CMML, detection of atrial fibrillation after stroke, prediction of bleeding and cardiovascular outcomes, and drug safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

clinical predictionEHRdecision supportexplainability

Trustworthy and secure AI

Adversarial machine learning, red teaming and jailbreak testing of large language models, prompt attacks on retrieval-augmented (RAG) systems, fairness evaluation of LLMs, and the explanation of anomalies and model errors.

LLM securityRAGadversarial MLfairness

AI Scientist

AI systems that automate parts of the scientific process, from AutoML and meta-learning that choose algorithms and pipelines automatically, as in AutoGRD (best paper, CIKM 2019) and TRIO (best paper runner-up, ICDM 2021), to autonomous AI agents that generate hypotheses, run analyses and report results.

AI scientistAutoMLmeta-learningAI agents

Recommender systems

Personalization at scale: context-aware, cross-domain, session-based and bundle recommendation, and transfer learning between domains. Several of these systems run on e-commerce sites with millions of users.

personalizationtransfer learningcontext

AI for the life sciences and drug discovery

Molecule generation with transformers and reinforcement learning, prediction of drug–drug interactions and drug withdrawal, bacterial sRNA–target interactions, antimicrobial resistance, and machine-learning analysis of cancer genomes.

molecular generationbioinformaticsAMR

Ensemble learning and decision forests

Methods that combine many models into one accurate and stable predictor: how forests are built and diversified, how they can be compressed, and how a forest can be turned into an interpretable tree.

ensemblesdecision treesboostinginterpretability

Other active topics: anomaly detection, security of mobile and database systems, time-series learning, Hebrew NLP for legal and police documents, and computational criminology.
